(Turnip, Mesa 26.2.99) and settings as the first logs of the same day.

## Findings

1. With a warm driver cache and a clean exit, upstream already avoids compile stutter for
   content seen before. The only compile stalls left were for content not seen before
   (4 long frames, worst 304 ms).
2. Most remaining long frames are in the first 15 s after boot or after a save state load,
   with the emulation thread busy (work time about equal to the interval) and no shader or
   pipeline activity. Cause not yet identified. Candidates to test: CPU JIT compilation of
   game code on first execution, and file reads.
3. One 1.24 s stall at 23.7 s in the first session, with no compile, pipeline wait or
   presentation wait involved.
4. Frame intervals on the emulation thread are quantised to the 120 Hz display period
   (8.33 ms): about 42% of frames take 8.3 ms, 14% take 16.7 ms and 42% take 25 ms. Frame
   presentation (`SwapBuffers`) blocks for a median of 15.8 ms on about two thirds of frames.
   Whether this uneven cadence reaches the screen is not known: these timestamps are from
   the emulation thread, not from the presentation thread.
5. The save-on-kill scenario was not exercised: the killed session lasted 5 s and compiled
   nothing new, so nothing was lost.
